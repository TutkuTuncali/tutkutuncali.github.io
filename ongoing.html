Research in progress · Manuscript in preparation

Contextual and Technological Variability of Grey Monochrome Ceramics at Daskyleion

This study investigates the variability of grey monochrome ceramics from Daskyleion, northwestern Anatolia, through quantitative analysis of macroscopic ceramic attributes and archaeological contexts. It examines how differences in spatial and stratigraphic resolution affect the identification and interpretation of ceramic patterns.

The research evaluates alternative analytical units, contextual uncertainty, and methods for comparing archaeological assemblages while preserving the relationship between material observations and archaeological interpretation.

02 / 15

Research in progress · Manuscript submitted

Archaeological Information Systems and Research Data Governance: The Mount Nif Case

This research examines the development and adaptation of information systems for long-term archaeological excavation projects. Drawing on the Mount Nif Excavation, it investigates how heterogeneous archaeological records can be integrated into structured information systems while maintaining data provenance, contextual information, and evolving research requirements.

The study addresses the relationship between information-system design, archaeological workflows, and the long-term reliability of research data.

03 / 15

Research in progress · Manuscript under revision

Adaptive Museum Experiences through Reinforcement Learning

This research investigates reinforcement learning as a computational approach to personalised visitor experiences in archaeological museums. Using the Iznik Museum as a research context, the study explores how adaptive decision models can support the selection and sequencing of interpretative content according to visitor characteristics, preferences, and interactions.

The broader objective is to investigate the role of adaptive artificial intelligence in cultural heritage communication and visitor-oriented interpretation.

04 / 15

Funded research project · In progress

PeVRsona: Personality-Aware Adaptive Storytelling in Virtual Cultural Heritage

PeVRsona investigates the use of machine learning and behavioural information to adapt virtual reality narratives to individual users. The project explores whether interaction patterns, movement, and attention-related signals can support personalised cultural heritage experiences without relying exclusively on conventional questionnaires.

The research combines adaptive storytelling, user modelling, immersive technologies, and responsible approaches to personalisation.

Supported by Marmara University through the ADEP Research University Support Programme. Research team member.

05 / 15

Collaborative research initiative · In progress

Mapping Digital Archaeology Practices in Türkiye

This initiative contributes to the systematic identification and documentation of digital methods used in archaeological research in Türkiye. Developed through the Computer-Assisted Archaeology Working Group of the Digital Cultural Heritage Network (DKM), the work brings together published research and information on digital archaeological practices.

The initiative seeks to improve the visibility, discoverability, and comparative understanding of computational approaches, research infrastructures, and digital technologies used in archaeology.

06 / 15

Research in progress · Manuscript under revision

Forecasting-Driven Replenishment Decisions in Multi-Level Distribution Systems

This research investigates how demand forecasts can be translated into operational replenishment decisions in distribution systems. It examines the relationship between predictive models, decision timing, inventory constraints, and the practical consequences of forecast-based ordering.

The study combines predictive analytics with operational decision modelling to evaluate how forecasting information can support replenishment decisions under realistic business constraints.

Developed with undergraduate project student Şevval Aslan as an output of her graduation project.

07 / 15

Research in progress · Manuscript submitted

ESG-Related Supplier Risk Assessment through DEMATEL

This study investigates environmental, social, and governance-related supplier risks through a multi-criteria decision-making framework. Using DEMATEL, it examines the interdependencies between risk criteria and identifies their relative influence within the supplier-risk system.

The research also evaluates the robustness of the resulting causal structure under alternative analytical assumptions and sensitivity scenarios.

08 / 15

Research in progress · Manuscript preparation

Geographical and Morphometric Variation in Crocus pallasii Pollen

This study examines geographical variation in the pollen morphology of Crocus pallasii using quantitative morphometric analysis across multiple populations. It investigates which pollen characteristics show spatial differentiation and which remain comparatively stable across geographical and environmental conditions.

The research applies multivariate statistical methods to evaluate population-level variation, geographical structure, and the interpretation of morphological diversity.

09 / 15

Research in progress · Manuscript preparation

Cross-Cultural Adaptation and Psychometric Evaluation of the Turkish VisAWI-Pos

This research examines the psychometric properties of the Turkish adaptation of the Visual Aesthetics of Websites Inventory, VisAWI-Pos. It evaluates the measurement structure, reliability, and validity of the adapted instrument through confirmatory and complementary psychometric analyses.

The study contributes to the assessment of perceived website aesthetics in Turkish-language research contexts and considers the methodological implications of cross-cultural measurement adaptation.

Research in progress · Manuscript under editorial consideration

Wikipedia Editathons as Spaces of Post-Disaster Heritage Engagement

This study investigates participation in collaborative digital heritage activities following the 2023 earthquakes in Türkiye. Focusing on Wikipedia editathons, it examines participants’ motivations, experiences, and contributions to the documentation and communication of cultural heritage.

The research addresses how collaborative knowledge platforms can support participation, public engagement, and the production of heritage-related knowledge in post-disaster contexts.

Joint research with Nurdan Çayırezmez and Deniz Çit of the Digital Cultural Heritage Network (DKM).

Collaborative research proposal · In development

Digging Smarter: AI-Ready Archaeological Field Recording

Digging Smarter explores an offline-first, AI-assisted approach to archaeological field documentation. The proposed system aims to support the transformation of field notes, photographs, voice recordings, and other observations into structured, multilingual archaeological records.

The research focuses on preserving archaeological context, supporting expert oversight, and reducing the documentation burden while creating data suitable for subsequent computational analysis.

Research concept developed within the MAIA COST Action WG3 research network.

This research explores the relationship between acceptance of generative artificial intelligence and its actual use. The planned research investigates appropriate AI use, behavioural calibration, and the relationship between individuals’ stated intentions and their decisions in practical tasks.

Master's thesis research · TUBITAK 2210-C scholarship application

Modelling Road Transport Decarbonisation Policy Mixes in Türkiye through 2035

This research investigates the emission reduction potential of alternative road transport decarbonisation policies in Türkiye through 2035. It combines Logarithmic Mean Divisia Index (LMDI) decomposition with a dynamic vehicle fleet stock-flow model to examine the historical drivers of transport emissions and simulate future policy outcomes.

The proposed framework integrates vehicle fleet transitions, alternative fuel technologies, energy consumption, electricity demand, charging infrastructure, and policy interactions. Drawing on the United Kingdom as a reference case, the research aims to evaluate alternative policy combinations and their potential effects under explicit assumptions and uncertainty.

The study is associated with an application to the TUBITAK 2210-C Domestic Priority Areas Master’s Scholarship Programme, under the priority area of Modelling and Simulation Technologies.

Research student: Arda Üst. Academic supervisor: Tutku Tuncalı Yaman.

SAYZEK Academic Thesis Programme 2026–2027 · Research application, Topic 43

Transforming Document Tables into Structured Knowledge Bases for Natural-Language Querying

This proposed research investigates methods for extracting tabular information from documents, transforming it into structured data, and enabling users to query the resulting information through natural language.

The project combines document processing, information extraction, database structuring, and Text-to-SQL approaches. Particular attention is given to evaluating query accuracy, identifying error sources, and assessing the reliability of generated outputs.

Developed in connection with Topic 43 of the SAYZEK Academic Thesis Programme, the proposed study addresses the relationship between unstructured documents, structured information systems, and natural-language access to data.

Research student: Nazlı Pirim. Prospective academic supervisor: Tutku Tuncalı Yaman.

Planned master's graduation project

Sustainability Disclosure, Financial Performance and Corporate Governance in BIST-Listed Banks

This planned research examines the relationship between corporate sustainability reporting, financial performance, and corporate governance among banks listed on Borsa Istanbul (BIST) during the 2023–2025 period.

The study proposes constructing a sustainability disclosure index and investigating its relationship with Return on Assets (ROA), Return on Equity (ROE), and corporate governance indicators. Particular attention will be given to changes in the sustainability reporting environment in 2024 and their implications for the comparability and interpretation of disclosures across reporting periods.

The research aims to distinguish changes in disclosure practices from potential relationships with financial and governance outcomes, using publicly available banking and sustainability reporting data.

Research student: Yiğit Ege Sarıtaş. Academic supervisor: Tutku Tuncalı Yaman.
